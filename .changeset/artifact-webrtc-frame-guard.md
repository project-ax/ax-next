Add `@ax/frame-guard`, a leaf library holding the in-page WebRTC and child-frame guard that chat visuals use, so artifact HTML can get the same lock from one shared implementation. The content origin now rewrites artifact HTML through it (frames and declarative shadow roots cut, the guard first in `<head>`) in a worker PROCESS of its own, never on the thread that serves requests and never in the process that has it: a heap limit V8 enforces on it, sized from the container's memory limit (30% of it, at most 384 MB; a container's limit ends every process in it under `memory.oom.group`, the host too, so the worker may take only a share), no environment, no standard streams, a wall-clock deadline, a short line of waiting pages, a busy rule, a check that it has the heap limit it was asked for and that what it answers is a page of at most 8 MiB with the guard in it, pages turned away for 30 s after five workers in a row that could not be started, the lower priority it asks for set on every thread of it (V8's helpers are made before it can ask), and an idle time after which it is ended to give its memory back. It holds against a worker that fails, not one that has been taken over. The rewrite bounds its own work too: parse depth, node, attribute, call and escaped-character counts, a result of at most four times the page and never over 8 MiB (of characters, and of bytes of UTF-8 where the worker makes them), strings read flat as they arrive so that the heap follows the biggest token, and a CPU-time budget. It decodes a UTF-16 page with a byte order mark, and answers a page it will not serve (or one over 4 MiB) with a 422 and one of three fixed lines (too large to preview, can't show it right now, or can't show it safely) instead of the 404 a bad token gets; the reason is logged at warn as a code. A refusal about the page is remembered by content hash in a cache of its own, so refusals never push a served page out (a worker timeout, a wall-clock deadline, an out-of-memory refusal, which a page at the edge of the limit or a machine short of memory can give a good page, and a crash with the page in hand, which something other than the page can also cause: for a minute only, on a monotonic clock). The `artifact_publish` descriptor names the limits.
