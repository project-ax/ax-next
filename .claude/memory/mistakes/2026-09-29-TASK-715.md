| 2026-09-29 | The card (and its parent's note) framed the leak as "the placeholder in the sandbox env" and offered a rate cap or an env scrub as the cheap first step. Neither bounds dollars, and measuring turned up two bigger things the card never mentioned: bytes written after the CONNECT reached the provider with the key around the request framer, and the key worked for the provider's whole API (Batch, Managed Agents, Files). | Measure the leak in the harness before choosing a control, and list every place the secret is substituted before designing a gate: a gate on the path you know is walked around by the path you did not read. |

Mutate back to the pre-fix behaviour, not to "the fix, switched off". |
| 2026-09-29 | Assumed `inner.destroy()` in a test would make the proxy see the client hang up. The real finding under it was a production leak: the proxy-side TLS wrapper reports `end` but never `close` when a client vanishes. | When a test cannot make the thing happen, find out whether production can make it happen before fixing the test. Here it could. |
| 2026-09-29 | Counted responses in a test with `/OK/g` after a `200 OK` whose body was also `OK`, so one response satisfied "two responses". | Count a response by its status line, not by a word the body might repeat. |
