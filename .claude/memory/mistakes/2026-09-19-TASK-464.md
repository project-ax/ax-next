# TASK-464 — `AlertTitle` renders `<h5>`, and TASK-446's outline check will catch it

The obvious way to write the new failure state was
`<AlertTitle>We couldn’t load your list</AlertTitle>` over an
`<AlertDescription>`. It typechecks, the panel's own 13 tests went green, and
`ConnectorsTab.test.tsx` — three directories away — went red:

```
h2 ("Sites we read without asking") skips to h5 ("We couldn’t load your list")
```

`packages/channel-web/src/components/ui/alert.tsx` hardcodes `AlertTitle` to
`<h5>` with no `asChild` / element override. Any `<Alert>` with a title, dropped
into a section whose own heading is an `h2` or `h3`, is a level skip that
`headingOutlineProblems` (TASK-446, `src/test-utils/heading-outline.ts`) reports.

Two things to take from it:

- **Run the surrounding surface's tests, not just the component's.** The panel
  test file cannot see the outline it is embedded in. The check that objects
  lives with whatever composes the component.
- **The content rule already agreed.** `lib/read-register.ts` says an error
  alert gets a title only when the surface holds positive evidence for the claim
  the title makes, and adds: "The naive tidy-up is to give every alert a
  heading. Do not." Here the section's `h2` already names the thing. Dropping the
  title was the right call on content grounds and the outline was the second
  vote, not the only one.

If a future alert genuinely needs a heading inside a nested section, the fix is
to give `ui/alert.tsx` an element override via the shadcn CLI — not to hand-roll
a styled `<div>` beside it, and not to loosen the outline check.

## Smaller one, same PR

The revoke notice lived **inside** the `<Card>`. Once the unknown state replaced
the whole Card, a revoke that succeeded followed by a re-read that failed swapped
the Card out and took the confirmation with it — somebody who had just clicked
"Ask again" got no word on whether it worked. The notice reports what the DELETE
did, which is a fact about the revoke and not about the list, so it belongs to
the panel and outside both branches. That is the #611 lesson one level up: it was
already hoisted out of the row, and needed hoisting out of the container too.
