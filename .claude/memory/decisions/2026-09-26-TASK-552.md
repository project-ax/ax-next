| 2026-09-26 | TASK-552: the abort signal reaches a subscriber as a required THIRD handler argument, `SubscriberHandler(ctx, payload, { signal })` (`SubscriberInvocation`), not as a field on `AgentContext`. Supersedes TASK-514's "abandoned, not cancelled — AgentContext has no abort signal" row: a timed-out subscriber is now abandoned AND told to stop. | The ctx flows on into every service call a subscriber makes; a `ctx.signal` would read as a contract services honour (none do) and would need a ctx copy per subscriber (identity change, wider surface). A third arg is scoped to exactly the subscriber the bus is giving up on, and existing 2-arg handlers stay assignable. | `AgentContext.signal` (card's first suggestion; wider, leaks into services); a signal in `FireOptions` from the caller (per-fire, would abort every subscriber at once, and the caller has no per-subscriber clock). |
| 2026-09-26 | TASK-552: one fresh `AbortController` per subscriber per fire, on the unbounded path too (never aborted there). Aborted only at `subscriberTimeoutMs`, with `DOMException('…', 'TimeoutError')` (same as `AbortSignal.timeout`), AFTER the deadline resolves. | Handlers can rely on `signal` existing; a shared never-aborting signal would accumulate listeners forever. Allocation only, no extra microtask hop, so TASK-514's load-bearing unbounded ordering is untouched. | Shared static signal (listener leak); `undefined` on unbounded fires (every honouring subscriber needs a null check). |
| 2026-09-26 | TASK-552: a late throw that IS the bus's own abort reason (identity check) logs `hook_subscriber_aborted` at debug instead of `hook_subscriber_failed`.
