## 2026-09-19 — TASK-447 Activity vs "Brought to you": the count was right, the feed was missing rows

- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447) — **The card's central framing was half REFUTED, and the refuted half was its prescribed fix.** It read the disagreement as a counting bug and asked that "the count be derived from the same query/collection the feed renders". That is not satisfiable and would not be desirable. `GET /api/workspace/activity` merges **two** sources — `routines:recent-fires-for-agent` and `decisions:recent-receipts-for-agent` — so its length is not a decision count in any deployment with routines; it pages newest-first on `resolved_at`, while `decisions:count` is a 7-day window on `created_at` that names no status. A `pending` decision is legitimately counted with nothing to show. The two measure **raised** vs **settled** and both readings are correct.
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447) — **Which surface was wrong: the feed.** `decisions:count` ships its definition sentence beside the integer — *"Decisions this agent raised for you in the last 7 days, whatever you decided — including the ones that expired."* — and the query matches it exactly. The defect was `RECEIPT_STATUSES` in `packages/decisions/src/receipts.ts`: a hand-written allow-list of `executed | approved-pending-agent | failed`. The two settled statuses it omitted were `dismissed` and `expired`, i.e. every outcome where **nothing ran**. So Activity showed every decision the person said yes to and none of the ones they said no to — the record of their own refusals was the part they could not see. *Alternatives:* narrow the counter to match the feed (REJECTED — it would make the number contradict its own shipped sentence, and undo TASK-266's single status-free query); declare both right and only document the distinction (REJECTED — a declined decision genuinely is an event that happened to the person, and the renderer was already built for it).
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447) — **The half-wired tell that settled the intended semantic.** `ActivityKind` in `packages/channel-web/src/lib/workspace-types.ts` has carried `'dismissed'` since AW-9 (`5b239815`), and `ActivityFeed.tsx`'s `KIND` map gives it an `X` icon and `text-muted-foreground`. **No producer ever emitted it.** The feed was designed to show declines; only the receipt derivation excluded them. That is evidence, not taste — it is why "include them" beat "document the distinction".
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447) — **Derived, not enumerated.** `RECEIPT_STATUSES` is now `DECISION_STATUSES.filter(s => !OPEN_STATUSES.includes(s))` and `receiptFor` is an **exhaustive `switch`** over `DecisionStatus` with a `never` arm. An allow-list of what to INCLUDE fails **open** — the row it forgets is invisible and there is nothing on the surface to say one went missing. The complement fails **closed at compile time**: a new status with no answer stops the build. `OPEN_STATUSES` moved from a module-local in `store.ts` (and a third copy in `__tests__/fake-store.ts`) to `types.ts`; `DecisionStatus` and `DecisionStatusSchema` are both derived from one `DECISION_STATUSES` tuple.
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447) — **`expired` came along, and NOT as scope creep.** Excluding it would have required literally writing `.filter(s => s !== 'expired')` — reintroducing the enumerated drop the card was filed about, for a status the counter's own sentence advertises by name. Two sentences, not one: a `dismissed` row carries its per-row `dismissedText` ("You turned this down…", authored at hold time), an `expired` row gets a **constant** `EXPIRED_RECEIPT`. `dismissedText` claims a choice, and nobody turned an expired decision down — attributing a choice to someone who never made one is H1 aimed at the person it misrepresents.
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447) — **A second fail-open, found while wiring the first.** `receiptToActivityEvent` keyed three ternaries off `r.outcome === 'failed'`, so *everything else* rendered as an approval — correct for the three outcomes that existed, a lie the moment a fourth arrived. Replaced with `RECEIPT_RENDERING`, a `Record` **total** over the outcome union (kind, badge, fenced-away fallback), and an unknown outcome is dropped rather than guessed at (the plugin is duck-typed across the bus, I2). The per-outcome **fallback** is the load-bearing field: `approvedText`/`dismissedText` can fence away to nothing, and the single old fallback would have printed *"You approved this."* over a decision the person declined — the exact derived-from-the-wrong-outcome lie those two strings are kept apart to prevent.
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447) — **`DECISION_RECEIPT_TAG = 'Approval'` is no longer the badge for every receipt.** Right for a row that follows a yes, including a failed one (they did approve; the attempt is what went wrong). Wrong over "You turned this down" and "This one ran out of time", where a badge saying *Approval* contradicts the sentence directly beneath it. Those two take `DECISION_UNRESOLVED_TAG = 'Decision'` — still one vocabulary answering one question (where did this row come from), with the outcome left to the row's own sentence and icon.
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447) — **TASK-444 (the "Not now" declined grant) does NOT gate this, and the check was cheap.** "Not now" is `GRANT_REJECT_LABEL` on `PermissionCard`/`GrantRow` — the capability-**grant** surface, whose deferral-vs-deny ambiguity is the open product call. A `Decision` is an outward **action** with `ghostLabel: "No — I'll handle it"`, an unambiguous deny, and `PermissionCard.tsx`'s header comment exists precisely to keep the two apart. Different surface, different verb, no dependency.
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447, review round 1 — **APPROVE**, 2 Minor + 2 Nit, three taken) — **The sharpest finding was that the PR had not applied its own thesis to the outcome list.** I derived `DecisionStatus` and `RECEIPT_STATUSES` from one `DECISION_STATUSES` tuple and then left `DecisionReceiptOutcome` and `DecisionReceiptSchema`'s `z.enum` as two hand-written copies — and the schema's `as unknown as z.ZodType<DecisionReceipt>` cast means **TypeScript never compares them**. That enum is `returns` for `decisions:recent-receipts-for-agent`, and a `z.object` drops what it does not declare, so an outcome present in the union and in `receiptFor` but missing from the enum stops arriving on the bus, silently. That is this card's own defect one layer down, inside the fix for it. Now both derive from `DECISION_RECEIPT_OUTCOMES`, with two sentinels: the lists match, and a real row of each of the five shapes survives `DecisionReceiptSchema.parse` unchanged. *Lesson:* when a change's whole argument is "derive, don't hand-list", grep the diff for every other hand-list it touches before opening the PR — a cast is where the compiler stops helping.
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447, review round 1, Nit — taken, and it was the same class as the bug) — **The unknown-outcome drop I added was silent.** Dropping is right (an outcome we cannot name must not render as an approval), but a row vanishing with nothing said is exactly the shape this card exists to remove, and I had written the drop into a file whose every other degraded read logs. `receiptToActivityEvent` now takes an optional `onUnknownOutcome` and the route passes `initCtx.logger`, keeping the mapper pure for its unit tests.
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447, review round 1, Nit — taken) — **`failed`'s fenced-away fallback was `DECISION_FALLBACK_APPROVED`.** Unreachable today and still wrong twice: "You approved this." is true but the wrong fact to lead a failure with, and the obvious replacement ("it did not work") is a *claim* — one kind of `failed` is the host dying mid-flight (TASK-253), where the call may well have gone out. The fallback now claims nothing about the action at all: our own sentence was what could not be printed, and saying so is true either way.
- `2026-09-19` (TASK-447, review round 1, Minor — a PR-prose error, not a code one) — **I reasoned about `z.enum` against zod 3.23.8; `^3.23.8` resolves to 3.25.76 in this workspace.** No behavioural difference for a readonly `as const` tuple, and `tsc --build` was clean either way — but the version in the PR body was one nobody has installed. Check the resolved version, not the range, before writing a compatibility claim into a PR.
