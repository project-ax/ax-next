- **To serialize a read-then-write against another writer when the row to lock may not exist yet, take `pg_advisory_xact_lock` as the transaction's first statement, in a statement of its own, and set READ COMMITTED on that transaction explicitly** (`db.transaction().setIsolationLevel('read committed')`). A lock taken inside the working statement doesn't help: the statement's snapshot is taken before the wait. Under REPEATABLE READ or SERIALIZABLE, even a lock in its own statement doesn't help, because the transaction's single snapshot is taken by that first statement, before the wait. Take a `Transaction<DB>`, not a `Kysely<DB>`: on the pool, the xact lock is released as soon as it is taken. To test it deterministically, a test session holds a row or the lock, and the test polls `pg_blocking_pids` until the other side is waiting. No sleeps. To pin the isolation level, use a second `pg.Pool` whose `connect` handler sets `SET SESSION CHARACTERISTICS AS TRANSACTION ISOLATION LEVEL REPEATABLE READ`. Write one test per lock-taker, and mutate each guard on its own: a mutant that drops two guards at once proves only that one of them is tested (TASK-1182, channel-slack `lockSwitch`, 2026-11-20).
